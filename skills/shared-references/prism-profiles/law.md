# Discipline Profile: Law / Legal Studies

## ID
`law`

## Research Methodology

- **Primary methods**: Doctrinal analysis (法教义学), legal interpretation decision tree (文义 -> 体系 -> 历史 -> 目的), normative analysis, comparative law, law and economics, empirical legal studies, case study, historical-institutional analysis
- **Evidence types**: Statutory text, judicial decisions (case law / 判例), legislative history, regulatory materials, comparative jurisdiction analysis, legal commentary (学说), empirical data (court statistics, survey data), economic modeling
- **Validation standards**: Doctrinal coherence with existing legal framework, logical consistency of normative arguments, coverage of relevant precedents and statutes, feasibility of proposed reforms, comparative law support, consistency with constitutional principles
- **Claim structure**: "Current rule X leads to problem Y; reform Z achieves better outcome because of reason W" or "Doctrine A should be interpreted as B based on statutory purpose, comparative evidence, and policy rationale C"

## Legal Interpretation Protocol

Source note: adapted as a workflow heuristic from 沐轩, "法律解释方法（文义、体系、历史、目的）如何组合使用？一张决策树帮你选", WeChat article, https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/Kttxc1LtSSOrlHaddmoQhw. Treat it as a method prompt, not as legal authority; cite doctrinal scholarship, statutes, cases, and legislative materials when using it in a paper.

When analyzing a statutory or rule-interpretation issue, do not list interpretation methods as parallel options. Apply them as a sequential decision tree:

| Step | Method | Trigger | Output |
|------|--------|---------|--------|
| 1 | Textual / literal interpretation (文义解释) | Start with the ordinary, technical, and legal meaning of the provision's words | If the wording resolves the dispute without contradiction or absurdity, stop here. The possible textual meaning is the boundary of interpretation. |
| 2 | Systematic interpretation (体系解释) | The text supports more than one plausible reading | Compare neighboring provisions, the statute as a whole, related statutes, rule hierarchy, and doctrinal structure. Prefer the reading that avoids conflict, redundancy, or gaps. |
| 3 | Historical interpretation (历史解释) | Systematic analysis still leaves competing readings | Use drafts, amendment history, explanatory reports, deliberation records, predecessor rules, and the typical scenario the lawmaker addressed. |
| 4 | Purposive / teleological interpretation (目的解释) | Historical meaning is outdated, unworkable, unjust, or in tension with the provision's objective function today | Ask what interest the rule protects, what conduct it regulates, and how that purpose should apply to current conditions while staying within legal limits. |

Operational rules:
- **No skipping**: Do not jump directly to legislative intent or statutory purpose without first showing why textual and systematic analysis are insufficient.
- **Loop-back allowed**: If systematic, historical, or purposive analysis exposes a contradiction in the initial textual reading, return to the earlier step and refine the competing meanings.
- **Write the trigger**: Each interpretation paragraph should state why this method is being used, what legal material it examines, which competing reading it rejects, and what doctrinal consequence follows.
- **Keep method and reform separate**: Interpretation of current law (解释论) may support a reading within the legal text; reform proposals (立法论) must be labeled separately.
- **Apply legality constraints**: For criminal, sanctioning, tax, administrative-penalty, or rights-restricting provisions, do not use purposive reasoning to create an analogy or expand liability beyond legally possible wording. Check constitutional principles, legality, proportionality, and rule hierarchy.

## Sub-fields and Methods

| Sub-field | Primary Method | Key Evidence |
|-----------|---------------|--------------|
| Constitutional Law (宪法学) | Doctrinal + normative | Constitutional text, constitutional court decisions, comparative constitutions |
| Civil Law (民法学) | Doctrinal analysis (法教义学) | Civil Code provisions, judicial interpretations (司法解释), guiding cases (指导性案例) |
| Criminal Law (刑法学) | Doctrinal + case analysis | Criminal Code, SPC interpretations, typical cases |
| IP Law (知识产权法) | Doctrinal + comparative + economic | IP statutes, WIPO treaties, patent/trademark databases, industry data |
| Sports Law (体育法) | Interdisciplinary (law + policy + economics) | Sports legislation, CAS awards, league regulations, comparative sports governance |
| Law and Economics (法经济学) | Economic modeling + empirical | Game theory models, regression analysis, natural experiments, cost-benefit analysis |
| Comparative Law (比较法) | Comparative institutional analysis | Multi-jurisdiction statutes, case law, legal transplant analysis |
| Legal History (法制史) | Historical-institutional | Archival sources, historical statutes, legislative records |
| International Law (国际法) | Treaty interpretation + customary law | Treaties, ICJ decisions, state practice, opinio juris |

## Paper Structure

### Doctrinal Analysis Paper (法教义学论文)
```
1. Introduction / Problem Statement (引言 / 问题的提出) — 1-2 pages
2. Doctrinal Framework (理论基础 / 教义学框架) — 3-4 pages
3. Normative Analysis of Current Law (现行法规范分析) — 3-4 pages
4. Comparative Law Perspective (比较法考察) — 2-3 pages
5. Case Analysis / Judicial Practice (判例分析 / 司法实践) — 2-3 pages
6. Reform Proposal (制度完善建议) — 2-3 pages
7. Conclusion (结论) — 1 page
```

### Law and Economics Paper (法经济学论文)
```
1. Introduction / Problem Statement — 1-2 pages
2. Institutional Background (制度背景) — 2-3 pages
3. Literature Review (文献综述) — 2-3 pages
4. Theoretical Model / Analytical Framework (理论模型 / 分析框架) — 3-4 pages
5. Empirical Analysis or Case Application (实证分析 / 案例验证) — 3-4 pages
6. Policy Implications (政策启示) — 2-3 pages
7. Conclusion — 1 page
```

### Comparative Law Paper (比较法论文)
```
1. Introduction / Problem Statement — 1-2 pages
2. Comparative Methodology (比较法方法论) — 1 page
3. Jurisdiction A Analysis (国别/地区A) — 3-4 pages
4. Jurisdiction B Analysis (国别/地区B) — 3-4 pages
5. Comparative Synthesis (比较分析与评价) — 2-3 pages
6. Implications for China (对中国的启示) — 2-3 pages
7. Conclusion — 1 page
```

### Citation Style
- Chinese journals: footnote style (脚注), GB/T 7714 — **详见 `../citation-cn-footnote.md`**
- US law reviews: Bluebook citation
- UK/EU journals: OSCOLA
- Interdisciplinary: Author-date (APA) when crossing into social sciences
- **中文论文默认**: 脚注（页下注），全文连续编号；具体格式因期刊而异，以 `citation-cn-footnote.md` 中的期刊速查表为准

## Review Dimensions

| Dimension | Weight | Description |
|-----------|--------|-------------|
| Problem Significance | 15% | Does the research address a genuine legal problem with theoretical or practical importance? |
| Doctrinal Rigor | 25% | Is the legal analysis grounded in correct interpretation of statutes, cases, and doctrines? |
| Argument Quality | 25% | Is the normative reasoning logically coherent, well-structured, and persuasive? |
| Comparative Depth | 10% | Does comparative analysis go beyond surface description to functional comparison? |
| Practical Relevance | 10% | Are reform proposals feasible and connected to China's legal reality? |
| Literature Coverage | 10% | Does the paper engage with the major scholarly debates in the field? |
| Writing Quality | 5% | Is the legal writing precise, structured, and free of ambiguity? |

## Discipline-Specific Rules

- **Always ground in positive law**: Every normative argument must reference specific statutory provisions or judicial decisions, not just abstract principles
- **China focus**: Research conclusions must ultimately be applicable to or engage with the Chinese legal system (中国实践)
- **Distinguish lege lata vs. lege ferenda**: Clearly separate analysis of current law (解释论) from proposals for law reform (立法论)
- **Case analysis must be systematic**: Do not cherry-pick cases — show the full picture of judicial trends
- **Avoid "comparative law tourism"**: Foreign law comparison must serve the analytical purpose, not just pad the paper
- **Interdisciplinary rigor**: When using economic or sociological methods, meet the standards of those disciplines, not just borrow vocabulary
- **Legislative history matters**: For Chinese law, trace the evolution of provisions through drafts and revisions
- **Acknowledge normative positions**: Legal scholarship is inherently normative — be transparent about your value commitments

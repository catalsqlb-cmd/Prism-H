# Discipline Profile: Management / Business

## ID
`management`

## Research Methodology

- **Primary methods**: Survey research (SEM, CFA, regression), case study (single/multiple/comparative), archival/secondary data analysis, experiments (lab/field), grounded theory, event study, content analysis, meta-analysis, simulation
- **Evidence types**: Survey data with structural equation modeling, panel data regression, event study CARs, interview transcripts, financial/accounting data, patent data, case narratives, archival organizational data
- **Validation standards**: Construct validity (convergent + discriminant), common method variance checks, endogeneity controls (Heckman, IV, PSM), measurement model fit indices (CFI, RMSEA, SRMR), multi-level analysis when appropriate, robustness checks
- **Claim structure**: "We theorize and find that X positively affects Y (β=A, p<B), mediated by M and moderated by W"

## Paper Structure

### Theory-Testing Empirical Paper
```
1. Introduction (2-3 pages)
2. Theory and Hypotheses (4-6 pages) — theory development with hypothesis derivation
3. Methods (3-4 pages) — sample, measures, analytical approach
4. Results (3-4 pages) — descriptive statistics, correlations, main tests, robustness
5. Discussion (2-3 pages) — theoretical contributions, practical implications, limitations
6. Conclusion (0.5-1 page)
```

### Theory-Building Paper (Qualitative)
```
1. Introduction (2-3 pages)
2. Theoretical Background (2-3 pages) — not hypotheses, but sensitizing concepts
3. Methods (3-4 pages) — research design, data sources, analysis approach
4. Findings (6-8 pages) — emergent themes with evidence
5. A Model / Framework (2-3 pages) — theoretical contribution
6. Discussion (2-3 pages) — contributions, boundary conditions, future research
```

### Citation Style
- APA 7th edition (Academy of Management and most management journals)
- Some journals use author-date with journal-specific formatting

## Discipline-Specific Rules

- **Theory is the main contribution**: Management papers are judged primarily on theoretical contribution, not just empirical findings
- **Hypotheses must be formally derived**: Each hypothesis needs a logical argument chain from theory to prediction
- **Endogeneity is the top concern**: Always discuss and address potential endogeneity (reverse causality, omitted variables, selection bias)
- **Common method variance**: If using single-source survey data, apply Harman's test, marker variable, or CFA approach
- **Measurement model before structural**: Report CFA results before testing hypotheses in SEM
- **Robustness is expected**: At minimum: alternative measures, alternative specifications, subsample analysis
- **Boundary conditions**: Discuss when and where the theory applies (and doesn't)
- **Dual contribution**: Best papers contribute to both theory and practice

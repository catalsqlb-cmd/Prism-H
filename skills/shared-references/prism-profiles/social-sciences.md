# Discipline Profile: Social Sciences

## ID
`social-sciences`

## Research Methodology

- **Primary methods**: Survey research, interviews (structured/semi-structured/in-depth), ethnography, content analysis, discourse analysis, case study (single/comparative), process tracing, mixed methods, grounded theory, experimental/quasi-experimental design
- **Evidence types**: Survey data (Likert scales, open-ended), interview transcripts, field notes, textual corpora, statistical analysis (descriptive + inferential), thematic coding, narrative evidence, archival data
- **Validation standards**: Sampling strategy and representativeness, inter-coder reliability for qualitative data, triangulation across methods, member checking, thick description for qualitative, effect sizes and confidence intervals for quantitative, IRB/ethics approval
- **Claim structure**: "Analysis of X reveals pattern Y, which challenges/supports theory Z" or "Through N interviews, we find that actors understand phenomenon P through lens Q"

## Paper Structure

### Quantitative Social Science Paper
```
1. Introduction (1-2 pages)
2. Literature Review and Hypotheses (2-3 pages)
3. Data and Methods (2-3 pages) — sample, measures, analytical strategy
4. Results (3-4 pages) — descriptive + multivariate
5. Discussion (2-3 pages) — interpretation, theoretical implications
6. Conclusion (1 page) — limitations, future research
```

### Qualitative Social Science Paper
```
1. Introduction (1-2 pages)
2. Literature Review / Theoretical Framework (2-3 pages)
3. Research Design and Methods (2-3 pages) — site, sampling, data collection, coding
4. Findings (5-7 pages) — thematic sections with rich evidence
5. Discussion (2-3 pages) — theoretical contribution
6. Conclusion (1 page)
```

### Mixed Methods Paper
```
1. Introduction (1-2 pages)
2. Literature Review (2-3 pages)
3. Research Design (1-2 pages) — mixed methods justification
4. Quantitative Phase (3-4 pages) — data, analysis, results
5. Qualitative Phase (3-4 pages) — data, analysis, findings
6. Integration and Discussion (2-3 pages)
7. Conclusion (1 page)
```

### Citation Style
- APA 7th edition (most social science journals)
- Chinese journals: GB/T 7714 or journal-specific format

## Review Dimensions

| Dimension | Weight | Description |
|-----------|--------|-------------|
| Research Question | 15% | Is the question theoretically motivated and socially significant? |
| Theoretical Framework | 20% | Is there a clear, well-grounded theoretical lens? |
| Methodological Rigor | 25% | Is the research design appropriate for the question? Are validity threats addressed? |
| Evidence Quality | 15% | Is the evidence sufficient, well-presented, and convincingly linked to claims? |
| Contribution to Literature | 15% | Does this advance theory or challenge existing understanding? |
| Ethical Considerations | 5% | Are research ethics properly addressed (consent, anonymity, reflexivity)? |
| Writing Quality | 5% | Is the paper clear, well-organized, and appropriately jargon-free? |

## Discipline-Specific Rules

- **Theory must frame the empirical work**: Every social science paper needs a clear theoretical framework — data without theory is mere description
- **Methods transparency**: Report enough detail for replication — sample size, selection criteria, interview guide, coding scheme
- **Reflexivity for qualitative work**: Acknowledge researcher positionality and its potential influence on findings
- **Operationalization matters**: Clearly define and justify how abstract concepts are measured or observed
- **Thick description**: Qualitative findings need rich, contextualized evidence — not just counts of themes
- **Limitations are expected**: An honest limitations section is a sign of rigor, not weakness
- **Positionality statement**: Increasingly required — state your relationship to the research context

# Discipline Profile: Humanities

## ID
`humanities`

## Research Methodology

- **Primary methods**: Hermeneutics (interpretive analysis), textual analysis / close reading, historical method (source criticism), critical theory, discourse analysis, genealogy, conceptual analysis, comparative analysis, archival research, philological analysis
- **Evidence types**: Primary texts (literary works, philosophical treatises, historical documents), archival materials, cultural artifacts, secondary scholarship, theoretical arguments, interpretive frameworks
- **Validation standards**: Internal coherence of interpretation, engagement with existing scholarly debates, textual fidelity (accurate reading of sources), historical contextualization, acknowledgment of alternative interpretations, theoretical sophistication
- **Claim structure**: "Reading X through lens Y reveals Z, which challenges the prevailing interpretation W" or "Historical analysis of P demonstrates that Q, reframing our understanding of R"

## Paper Structure

### Interpretive / Analytical Paper
```
1. Introduction — Problem and Thesis (1-2 pages)
2. Scholarly Context / Literature Review (2-3 pages)
3. Theoretical/Methodological Framework (1-2 pages)
4. Analysis Part I — Primary Argument (4-6 pages)
5. Analysis Part II — Supporting Argument or Counter-argument (3-4 pages)
6. Implications / Broader Significance (1-2 pages)
7. Conclusion (1 page)
```

### Historical Research Paper
```
1. Introduction — Question and Significance (1-2 pages)
2. Historiographical Review (2-3 pages)
3. Sources and Methodology (1-2 pages)
4. Historical Narrative / Analysis (8-12 pages, may have sub-sections)
5. Discussion — Broader Implications (2-3 pages)
6. Conclusion (1 page)
```

### Citation Style
- Chicago Manual of Style (Notes and Bibliography) — most common for humanities
- MLA for literature
- Chinese journals: GB/T 7714 or journal-specific footnote style

## Review Dimensions

| Dimension | Weight | Description |
|-----------|--------|-------------|
| Thesis Originality | 20% | Does the paper offer a genuinely novel interpretation or argument? |
| Scholarly Engagement | 20% | Does it engage seriously with existing debates and counterarguments? |
| Textual/Evidentiary Rigor | 20% | Are primary sources read accurately and contextualized properly? |
| Argumentative Coherence | 20% | Is the argument logically structured and internally consistent? |
| Theoretical Sophistication | 10% | Is the theoretical framework appropriate and well-applied? |
| Writing Quality | 10% | Is the prose clear, elegant, and appropriate for the discipline? |

## Discipline-Specific Rules

- **Interpretation over information**: The goal is not to report facts but to advance an interpretation or argument
- **Engage with the tradition**: Humanities papers must situate themselves in an ongoing scholarly conversation
- **Close reading is evidence**: Detailed textual analysis is the primary evidentiary method — do not merely summarize
- **Acknowledge complexity**: Resist oversimplification — good humanities scholarship embraces nuance and ambiguity
- **Footnotes carry weight**: Footnotes in humanities are not just citations — they contain substantive arguments, qualifications, and scholarly dialogue
- **Avoid scientism**: Do not force quantitative methods or "hypothesis testing" language onto interpretive work
- **Primary sources first**: Always read primary sources directly, not only through secondary scholarship
- **Historical sensitivity**: Avoid presentism — interpret historical texts in their own context before drawing modern implications
